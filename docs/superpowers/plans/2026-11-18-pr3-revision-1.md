# PR 3 revision 1: the owner's art and UX notes

Applies on top of `t3/pr3-porch-world` once the phase C review fixes land. Same global constraints as `2026-11-18-pr3-porch-world.md` (art rules, palette, `--px`, testids, motion, no shortcut text, no image files). The overarching note: **the menus must integrate into the environment.** Panels should feel like objects in the world (paper pinned to wood, chalk on a board, tickets, plaques), not floating app cards.

Two tracks that can run in parallel on separate branches, then merge:

- **Track P** (porch and Daily): branch `t3/pr3-rev1-porch`
- **Track S** (studio and rooms): branch `t3/pr3-rev1-studio`

Shared file risk: `PlayLayout`/`play.css` are used by both. Track P owns `PlayLayout`'s structure. Track S only adds room-specific slot content and must not restructure `PlayLayout`.

## Track P: porch and Daily

### P1. Boosters (easel and chalkboard)
The rockets look rough. Redraw the booster as a clean, small, readable sprite: a rounded metal nozzle body with a highlight and a dark outline, a trim band, a bell nozzle, and a flame in three palette tones (core, mid, tip) with a 3-frame flicker. All boosters share one sprite.

### P2. The cat
Add a sleeping "z" animation: small pixel "z" glyphs that rise and fade above the cat's head in a slow loop (frozen under reduced motion), alongside the existing breathing.

### P3. Today's painting on the porch easel
Once today's attempt has ended, the porch easel shows the player's actual painting (the best-graded canvas, rendered at pixel scale on the easel board) instead of the cover sheet. A small paper tag on the easel reads "Today's painting · <score>/1000", and clicking the easel opens the read-only result view (existing behaviour).

### P4. The Daily play screen sits in the environment
- **Backdrop:** a better close-up backdrop: a believable porch view behind the easel, with the railing, the beach strip and the sea with the sun reflection, a hint of the house wall at the far left, and the roof fascia at the top. The same light and palette as the porch, with richer detail.
- **Left column → a clipboard or paper pinned to the porch post.** The prompt, timer, auto-grade timeline and manual grades sit on a paper sheet clipped to a board hanging on the post (a visible clip and nail), and Exit becomes a small wooden "← Porch" sign below it.
- **Right column → the floating chalkboard.** "How you're scored" is chalk on the hovering chalkboard: the bars drawn in chalk, the total in large chalk numbers and the judge's line in chalk handwriting. Grade now and Submit & finish hang below the board as two wooden plaques or pegs. It keeps the boosters and the hover (still while the player is drawing, so the chalkboard doesn't bob during play either).
- **The painter's tray** is literally the easel's tray extended: a wooden shelf with the tools resting on it as objects (a brush, an eraser, a bucket, an eyedropper, undo/redo arrows carved into the wood) above a row of paint pots or blobs for the colours. Labels stay small but legible.
- Keep every testid and the layout bounds.

### P5. Mobile Daily (375×812): no scrolling
Everything fits in one viewport with no scroll and no drawer:
- **Top:** a slim prompt strip with the timer and Exit.
- **The canvas:** as large as possible (target at least 340 px wide, near edge-to-edge with a thin easel frame).
- **A compact tray:** a single row of six small tool icons (36 px, icons only, with labels as tooltips and aria-labels on mobile), plus colours in two rows of eight small pots (32 px minimum, with a gap; acceptable on mobile when spaced).
- **A score strip at the bottom:** best score, the next auto-grade countdown, and the Grade now / Submit buttons in one row. Tapping the score opens a compact chalkboard sheet with the breakdown, as an overlay, not page scroll.

Assert in tests: `documentElement.scrollHeight <= 812` and a canvas width of at least 340.

## Track S: studio and rooms

### S1. Clean up the basement
It's a mess right now. Simplify to a clear composition:
- **Back wall:** much bigger windows (two or three large windows taking most of the upper wall) looking out on the beach and the sunset sea.
- **Left half:** the room logic.
- **Right half:** the Magic Easel.
- **Fewer props:** a workbench on the left, a shelf, the stairs.
- **The stairs:** fix the handrail. It's a proper banister that follows the stair angle, with posts at the bottom and top and balusters along the stringer.

### S2. Room logic on the left
On the left, as in-world objects:
- **A rack or corkboard of room tickets:** one paper ticket per open public room (name, code, painter count), plus a special gold "Public board" ticket, pinned first. The tickets scroll inside the board if there are many.
- **"Got a code?":** a top hat on the workbench with a ticket slot beside it. Typing a code and pressing "Drop it in" animates the ticket dropping into the hat, sparkles burst from the hat, and the Magic Easel conjures that room.
- **"Set up an easel":** a small order pad on the workbench (name, public/private, "Conjure").
- Keep all testids: `room-card-*`, `room-card-play` (the gold ticket), `lobby-play-now`, `create-room-*`, `visibility-*`, `join-*` and `public-rooms-list`. The delete ("Pack up") control stays on the owner's ticket.

### S3. The Magic Easel on the right
- **Idle:** a single ornate easel with a blank canvas, a soft shimmer, floating sparkle particles and a gentle hover (with boosters, matching the porch family, plus a little magic glow).
- **Selecting a ticket or the public board** plays a transformation of about 900 ms: sparkles swirl, the canvas flips or wipes, and the easel resolves to a live preview of that room's board. The live cells come from a read-only spectator connection to the room (reuse the existing room client in spectator mode; the builder decides whether the lobby preview counts as a watcher). It shows the room name, painters, the round state and the word hidden. A "Step in" plaque (or Enter) goes to `/r/:code`.
- **Selecting another ticket** transforms again. Under reduced motion it's a crossfade.
- **Joining by code** runs the same transformation after the hat. An invalid code gives a puff of smoke and "No easel by that name".
- The public board isn't a wall painting any more; it's just the gold ticket that the Magic Easel can become. Remove the big framed painting of the window view.

### S4. A live, animated critic
The room critic (the cat in a beret) gets animation states: an idle blink and tail flick; "thinking" (a paw on chin and a "…" bubble while the AI is looking); "guess" (it leans in, the bubble pops with the guess); "correct" (it jumps, the beret flies, confetti); "resting" (asleep with a "z", matching P2). It's driven from the existing guess feed and round state. Frozen to a single pose under reduced motion.

### S5. Room side panels integrate
Following P4's approach: the room's left column (word, timer, players) is a paper sheet pinned to a studio pillar, and the right column is the critic on a stool beside a chalk slate for the guesses and the score note. "Ask the AI" is a bell on the stool, and the painter's tray is shared from Track P.

## Amendment: Haiku the cat, and no AI references

- **The critic is Haiku the cat, everywhere.** The porch cat sleeping on the chair is Haiku. In the Daily play screen, Haiku sits beside the easel (on the porch railing or a stool near the floating chalkboard) and does the grading: the thinking pose while a grade is in flight, then a speech bubble with the guess and the judge's one-line reason, then a happy reaction on a high score and a shrug on a low one. It uses the same animation states as S4 (shared sprite and component, owned by Track S; Track P mounts it in Daily). Under reduced motion it holds one pose.
- **Remove every user-facing AI reference.** No "AI", "Claude", "LLM", "model" or "judge" in visible copy, aria-labels, titles or SEO text. Haiku is a cat who guesses what you drew. Examples:
  - "Ask the AI" → "Ask Haiku"
  - "Haiku says" stays
  - "AI guessed it!" → "Haiku guessed it!"
  - "Draw together. Beat the AI." → "Draw together. Get Haiku to guess."
  - "Claude Haiku watches the canvas" → "Haiku the cat watches the canvas"
  - "Judge verdict" → "Haiku's verdict"
  - "judge resting" → "Haiku's napping, back tomorrow"
  - The scoring copy keeps its numbers ("Matches the prompt", "Easy to recognise", "Speed").
  Internal code names (`judge`, `ai`) don't change; only displayed strings, plus the testids' visible text where tests assert copy (update those assertions).
- **Ownership:** Track S owns the Haiku sprite and animation component (`src/components/haiku/Haiku.tsx`). Track P waits for it, or stubs a static Haiku and swaps it in at merge. The copy sweep runs across both tracks: each track updates the files it touches, and a final sweep after the merge uses `grep -rniE "\bAI\b|claude|llm|judge" src --include=*.tsx` on visible strings.
